---
title: Where Gramark is headed
description: Why Gramark's focus has shifted from proving a self-hosting engine to building the product around it — interchange-format credibility, a live Notebook, and a planned rebrand.
---

import { Aside, CardGrid, LinkCard } from "@astrojs/starlight/components";

Gramark started as an internal proof: could a grammar notation describe
itself well enough to bootstrap its own parser generator? That question is
answered — the self-hosting loop (lexer → canonical LR(1) → LALR/IELR →
generated codegen → parsing its own grammar spec back) is done and stable,
with an ALL(\*)/PEG side-engine and several Scala backend targets layered on
top of it since. The project's center of gravity has moved on to what that
engine is actually _for_. Three things are happening in parallel now.

## Earning credibility as an interchange format

Rather than asserting Gramark is "the standard" EBNF-family format, the plan
is to prove it — with real round-trips through established tools. ANTLR was
first; Bison/yacc is the one most recently finished. The bar isn't "it
parses": it's `import(export(x))` fidelity — a `.g4` or `.y` grammar
imported into Gramark and exported back out should be the same grammar, not
an approximation of it.

## Becoming a learning and debugging platform

Most of the recent work isn't in the CLI at all — it's the **Grimoire
Notebook**, a standalone, live-editing, in-browser surface where a grammar
is a real document: you edit it, get located error/warning squiggles on the
exact broken cell, see railroad diagrams with action badges for every rule,
and try it against real input via a calculator built from the grammar's own
`{% %}` actions. That's a materially different product shape than "run a
CLI, get a parser" — the Notebook is meant to be where you _understand_ a
grammar, not just where you compile one.

## A planned identity change to match

<Aside type="note" title="Not yet in effect">
  This is a planned, gated change — nothing on the site has been renamed yet. It
  ships as a full redesign before a general-availability release, not a
  find-and-replace.
</Aside>

The project intends to rebrand **Gramark → Grimoire**, specifically because
of this shift. "Grimoire" shares the root _gramaire_ with "grammar" — it
names a book you write living things into, which fits a live notebook in a
way the old grammar-plus-Markdown pun ("Gra-m-ark") never had to. The
Grimoire Notebook already carries the name today, ahead of the rest of the
rebrand, because it's the one part of the product this new identity was
coined to describe.

## Where this leaves the fundamentals

None of this changes what already works. The three parse-table methods
(canonical LR(1), LALR(1), IELR(1)), the single-file `.grmk.md` format, and
the self-hosting loop are the foundation the interchange-format work and the
Notebook are both built on — they're not being replaced, just no longer the
whole story.
